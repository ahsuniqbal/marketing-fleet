# Brand strategy

**Stage:** F.2a · **Agent:** brand-strategist
**Reads:** `foundation/intake.md`, `foundation/research.md`, `foundation/social-research.md`
**Writes:** `clients/<client>/foundation/brand-strategy.md` (template:
`templates/client-brand-strategy.md`)

Every headline, script, and ad angle has to answer to this document. Without it, each campaign
invents its own positioning and the client ends up sounding like five different brands.

## Procedure

1. **Positioning statement.** For <audience> who <need>, <brand> is the <category> that
   <primary benefit>, because <reason to believe>. Unlike <main alternative>, it <difference>.
   The difference must come from the research gaps, not from wishful thinking.
2. **Audience priorities.** Rank the personas from `research.md`: primary, secondary, and who
   the brand is explicitly **not** for.
3. **Messaging pillars — three to five.** Each pillar has: the message in one sentence, the
   persona it serves, the proof that backs it, and two or three example angles. A pillar without
   proof is an aspiration and is marked as such.
4. **Competitive frame.** How the brand is described relative to the alternatives, and which
   competitor territory to stay out of.
5. **Claims policy.** A table every copy decision is checked against:
   - **Allowed** — claims with proof on file (link the proof)
   - **Allowed with qualifier** — e.g. "up to", "in our 2025 customer survey"
   - **Not allowed** — unproven superlatives ("best", "#1"), guarantees, health or financial
     outcomes, competitor comparisons without evidence, and anything in the intake's do-nots
   - **Regulated** — category rules (health, finance, alcohol, children) and each ad platform's
     restricted-content policy that applies
6. **Proof bank.** Testimonials, numbers, certifications, press — each with its source and
   whether the client has permission to use it.
7. **Open questions** for the client, especially on claims needing proof the client has not
   supplied.

## Checklist

- [ ] Positioning difference traceable to a research gap
- [ ] Personas ranked, including who the brand is not for
- [ ] Three to five pillars, each with proof or marked aspirational
- [ ] Claims policy covers allowed, qualified, not allowed, regulated
- [ ] Intake do-nots copied into "not allowed"
- [ ] Proof bank items have source and usage permission
- [ ] Open questions listed

## Failure modes

- **Positioning everyone already owns.** "Quality and great service" differentiates nothing.
- **Too many pillars.** Seven pillars means no pillar.
- **No claims policy.** The copywriter writes "clinically proven" and nobody stops it.
- **Proof without permission.** A customer quote used without consent.
