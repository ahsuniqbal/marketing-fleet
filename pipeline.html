Agency pipeline · marketing-fleet v0.1.0

Marketing Fleet Pipeline

Ten role agents and twenty-seven skills carrying a client from the first call to launched, measured campaigns. Campaigns ship one at a time, per client, and every campaign starts from what the last one learned. Campaign 7 must sound and look like campaign 1 — and perform better.

10 agents 27 skills 23 stages 9 human gates 4-way parallel production

The three rules everything else is procedure

Agents have no memory between runs. An account lead carries the client's history in their head; an agent starts blank every invocation. These three rules are what replace that.

C.0 preamble

Every agent reads playbook.md before doing anything. What worked is reused; what failed is not repeated. Skipping it is how a campaign retests a hook that already lost.

Tokens and voice are law

No hardcoded color, font, or spacing in creatives or video — only tokens.json. No copy that contradicts voice-guide.md or the claims policy.

Humans hold the money and the publish button

Agents never launch ads, spend budget, post to social accounts, or send client email. They prepare import-ready files and a checklist; a person ships.

Phase A · Onboarding once per client · /onboard

Everything a campaign will lean on is decided here, once: who the client is for, how they sound, what they may claim, and what they look like — as tokens that both static creatives and video import.

F.0
account-manager
client-intake
Client notes become foundation/intake.md — said vs inferred kept separate, open questions listed with options, explicit out of scope.
Gate · Client Open questions answered before any research runs.
Parallel research

market-research
Market, competitors, personas, keywords — every claim with a source and a date. Its competitor list unblocks F.1b.

Gate · Client Positioning, pillars, claims policy, and voice approved.

Phase B · Campaign loop one campaign at a time · /campaign

Serial campaigns mean each one is measured before the next is planned, and the playbook compounds. The cost is volume — which is why the brand-reviewer at C.6 matters more here than anywhere else.

C.0
every agent
Read playbook.md, then CONVENTIONS and the foundation.
C.1
account-manager
campaign-brief
One roadmap entry becomes brief.md, with a measurable business outcome.
Gate · Client Brief and its success measure approved.
C.2
campaign-strategist
campaign-strategycontent-calendar
One goal, one primary KPI with a target that has a basis; channel, format, and budget mix from evidence. calendar.csv is the source of truth — no auto-posting.
Gate · You KPIs, mix, budget split, and calendar approved.
C.3
campaign-strategist
creative-brief
Every deliverable by ID: format, size, duration, message, pillar, CTA, owner, variants, due date.
Gate · You Creative brief frozen. This gate is what makes four-way parallel production safe.

Phase C · Report and close after the flight · /report

C.9
analytics-reporter
campaign-report
GA4 plus the Ads Manager / Google Ads exports in ads/results/. KPIs vs targets; winners and losers by creative, audience, format, hook. Missing data listed, inconclusive tests called inconclusive.
Gate · You Report reviewed before it reaches the client.
C.10
analytics-reporter
close-campaign
Writes summary.md (detail) and folds it into playbook.md (index, one line per learning with its evidence).
↺  The next campaign starts at C.0 by reading the playbook just written. That read is the entire payoff of C.10.

Before first use, fill in CONVENTIONS.md: agency, platforms in scope, sizes, copy limits, render settings, credit budgets, naming, approval gates. Any field left as TBD becomes a question agents must ask rather than a guess they will get wrong.
